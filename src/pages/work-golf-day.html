One deck. One golf day. Same handwriting.

Verlane Bank has a presentation template it loves and a client golf day coming up. Nobody wants to brief a new look for one afternoon. So the deck's line drawings become a course map and its typography goes onto the signs. A golfer can read the arrow and the hole number from a moving cart.

01 The reference

Start with what's already loved.

Before we design anything, we work out what makes the reference distinctive. In Verlane's deck, four things do the work. Together they are the creative language.

  • A single-weight line Every drawing in the deck uses it: hills, trees, icons.
  • One yellow dot The sun on the title slide, a marker beside every icon.
  • A serif with character Headlines only, and always large.
  • Navy, paper, one accent A lot of navy, and very little else.

02 The brief

A brief with nothing missing.

Six designs for one day, all from one deck. With the reference, the brand guidelines, final copy and the printer's specifications in hand, the job can be quoted and dated.

04 The edges

What we left alone.

An extension needs new design decisions, not a new idea. Three things stayed exactly where the brand left them.

  • A new logoVerlane's mark appears exactly as the brand guidelines set it.
  • A new colourNavy, paper and yellow. Nothing new invited in.
  • A new ideaA theme for the day would be idea work. That goes back to the lead agency.
