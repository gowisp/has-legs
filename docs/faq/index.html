FAQ

Fair questions.

Plain answers about what we do, how we use AI, when the clock starts and what it costs. If yours isn't here, put it in a brief or an email.

The basics

What is a creative extension?

A new piece designed in the creative language of work you've already approved. It needs new design decisions but no new idea: a deck's illustration style carried onto event signage, or three more pieces for a campaign that's already running.

What counts as a reference?

Any approved creative work that sets the look: a presentation template, a campaign poster, a report, an event identity. If your organisation has signed it off and people like it, it can be a reference.

What kinds of pieces do you make?

Whatever the job after the big idea needs: signs, maps, banners, backdrops, badges, scorecards, shelf strips, counter cards, invitations and screens. If it can be designed in the creative language of your reference, send the brief.

Can you come up with a new campaign for us?

No, and that's deliberate. A new campaign idea, an identity or a logo is idea work, and it belongs with your lead agency. We start where the idea is already approved.

Do you do resizes?

Not on their own. A resize refits an existing layout to new dimensions, with no design decisions to make. When resizes are part of a Set or a Kit, they come included.

AI and designers

Why wouldn't we just use AI ourselves?

You could. You could also service your own car: buy the parts, learn the engine, give up your weekend. Most people don't, because it isn't their job and the garage gets it right the first time. We keep up with the tools, we've built the workflows, and a designer signs off every file. You get on with more pressing matters.

Is a person doing this, or a machine?

Both, in that order. A designer works out what makes your reference distinctive, makes the design decisions and signs off every file. AI helps explore layouts and produce variations, which is where the speed comes from.

Will it look machine-made?

No. AI explores routes and produces variations. A designer chooses, corrects and finishes, then checks every file against your reference, your brand guidelines and the specifications before signing it off.

Turnaround

What do you need from us, and when do the 24 hours start?

Four things: the reference, your brand guidelines, final copy and the specifications for each piece. The clock starts once the brief is agreed and all four are in hand, and it runs on working days.

Does the 24 hours include weekends?

No. The clock runs on South African working days, so weekends and public holidays don't count. A brief agreed on Friday afternoon comes back on Monday afternoon.

What if we want changes?

One round of changes is included in the fee and comes back within another 24 hours. If you expect to need more, we agree that in the quote.

What about bigger jobs?

A Kit covers everything for one event or campaign, so it gets its own timing. We quote within one working day and give you a delivery date with the quote.

Your brand and your files

We don't have our brand guidelines to hand. Is that a problem?

Yes, but it's quickly fixed. Go straight back to your agency and ask for them. We don't guess at a brand or apply it at random. Once we have the guidelines alongside your reference, we can start.

Is our brand material safe with you?

Yes. Our designers work only in tools the studio has approved, set so that your material is not used to train AI models. Nothing we make for you is shown publicly without your permission.

Who owns the finished artwork?

You do, once it's paid for. We keep a copy in your brand file so that your next brief starts from what's already approved.

Will you show our work to other people?

Not without your permission. That's why the work on this site is demonstrations on invented brands.

Agencies

Will our agency mind?

We don't do idea work, so we aren't after their account. We take the jobs that are too small for an agency studio and too visible for PowerPoint. Agencies can also brief us themselves and deliver the work under their own name.

We're an agency. Can we put our name on it?

Yes. Brief us yourself and deliver the work under your own name. You manage the client relationship, and your client never sees us.

Will you approach our clients?

No. We never approach an agency's client about work the agency brought in, and idea work always goes back to the lead agency. If a client of yours finds us on their own, we take the brief, and the idea work still goes back to you.

Prices

How is the fee worked out?

It's a fixed fee, agreed before we start. It reflects how much new design is needed, the number of formats and the changes we've agreed. A Single starts from R2,400, and each further piece in a Set from R1,800.

Why do your prices say "from"?

Because a pocket scorecard and a stage backdrop aren't the same amount of design. The "from" price is where a straightforward piece starts. You get the exact fee, fixed, before the clock starts.

Words we use

Fourteen words, one meaning each.

A brief goes faster when everyone means the same thing. These are the words you'll hear from us, and exactly what we mean by them.

Reference
The approved creative work you bring. It sets the creative language for the job.
Brand guidelines
Your brand's own rules for logo, colour and type. We need them alongside the reference, because we don't guess at a brand.
Extension
A new piece designed in the creative language of a reference. New design decisions, no new idea.
Resize
An existing layout refitted to new dimensions. Never sold alone, always included in a Set or a Kit.
Idea work
A new campaign idea, identity or logo. Not ours. It goes back to your lead agency.
Brief
Your statement of what you need next: which pieces, for what use, by when.
Single
One extension, at a fixed fee.
Set
Up to five related extensions from one reference, at a fixed fee.
Kit
Everything for one event or campaign, from one reference, quoted per job.
Turnaround
The time from an agreed brief, with all four things in hand, to first finished artwork.
Brand file
Our record of your approved assets and the design decisions from earlier jobs, so your next brief starts there.
Referral
A job an agency passes on. We then work with the brand team directly.
White-label
A job an agency buys and delivers under its own name. The brand team doesn't see us.
Demonstration
An extension made on an invented brand to show the method. Always labelled as one.
